Our mission
We put real tools and a real problem-solving process in small hands.

So every child learns their ideas matter, and their hands can build them. Our vision is simple: raise problem-solvers, not answer-finders.

Machines are learning to answer. We teach children to ask, to notice, and to solve — and to care who it's for.

Arc is a design thinking club that happens to use 3D printers, not a 3D printing club. The machines are there to make a child's thinking visible.

Idea to Design

8-week course · 90 minutes weekly · max 12 children

Eight weeks, one real problem, one real person to solve it for. Your child leaves with a prototype they can explain — and a version two that proves they listened to feedback and changed their mind.

  • Finds a genuine problem belonging to someone in their life
  • Generates many solutions before committing to one
  • Prototypes with real tools, including 3D printing
  • Tests with the person it was made for, then rebuilds
  • Presents the finished work at an end-of-term showcase

In the room

What learning looks like here.

Not a showroom of finished pieces. This is the middle of the work — the testing, the rebuilding, and the week a plan stopped working.

No tests. No grades. We keep a weekly observation record across five criteria, a photographed portfolio from week one to week eight, an end-of-term showcase where your child presents to you, and a written report with at least one specific sentence about your child — not a template. The portfolio is the real evidence: a week-one attempt beside a week-eight finished piece says more than any score.

Who runs it

Built by a designer. Run by an educator.

Arc was not built by a franchise team writing lesson plans from a distance. It was built by a product designer who spent twelve years running design thinking sessions, and four years teaching children in Bangkok international schools.

That matters in the room. The process children work through here is the same process a working designer uses — cut down to a size a seven-year-old can hold, but never simplified into a craft activity with a predetermined outcome.

It also means someone is watching how each child works, not just what they made. That is what ends up in the written report.
